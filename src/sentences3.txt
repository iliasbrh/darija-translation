I love walking around the streets of the bournazel neighborhood in Casablanca where my dad grew up, and I have awesome memories of seeing my cousins in the family's house. I also reminisce about the trips all around Morocco, especially in essaouira, ouarzazate, marrakech and tangier. The route from France to Morocco all across Spain is also a good memory, if we forget the time when I drank a full espresso when i was eight years old.
I spent nearly forty-five minutes this morning arguing with the merchant at the weekly souk over the price of fresh mint and orange blossom water needed for the family gathering, entirely forgetting that my grandmother expected me back hours ago to help roll the semolina grains for the Friday couscous, which meant I had to hurriedly catch a shared grand taxi under the blazing midday heat just to avoid her legendary disapproval. 
After staring at the winding, hairpin turns of the Tizi n'Tichka mountain pass for over three hours while our old white grand taxi rattled like a loose metal cage, I finally decided to close my eyes and lean against the window, watching the rocky peaks of the High Atlas slowly disappear into a thick layer of winter fog that threatened to delay our arrival in Ouarzazate by several hours.
My cousin called me right when I was in the middle of grinding a massive bowl of toasted almonds and sesame seeds for the Ramadan sweets we decided to prep for the upcoming holy month, so I had to awkwardly balance the phone between my shoulder and my ear while wiping honey from my fingers and trying not to burn the next batch of chebakia in the heavy iron pot while she gossiped about our neighbor's elaborate engagement party.
The weather forecast kept insisting that the Atlantic breeze would remain calm all afternoon, so I confidently spread out our thick handmade wool rugs to dry on the rooftop terrace overlooking the ancient medina, only for a sudden, aggressive gust of the hot Chergui wind to blow in from the desert less than an hour later and absolutely cover every single fabric in a fine layer of red dust before I could sprint up the stairs to save them.
As the foreign diplomatic delegation spent three days analyzing the expanding industrial infrastructure of the Tanger-Med mega-port along the Strait of Gibraltar, they finally realized that Morocco's strategic positioning as a continental maritime hub was completely shifting the balance of trade between Europe and West Africa, forcing regional powers to negotiate new bilateral security agreements to safeguard the critical shipping lanes that connect the Mediterranean to global supply chains.
